Kampung Ratenggaro

A traditional village facing the ocean, at a river mouth.

Home/Discover Sumba/West/Kampung Ratenggaro

Why come here

Ratenggaro is one of Sumba's most photographed villages, and considered the oldest on the island: conical thatched-roof houses — called "Uma Mbatangu" — sometimes fifteen to twenty metres tall, lined up facing the Indian Ocean. These aren't reconstructions — families still live here under an ancestral social order, and megalithic tombs stand between the houses, evidence of a relationship with ancestors that's still very much alive. The villagers belong to the Kodi people, with their own language and traditions; many still chew areca nut and betel leaf daily, a habit that stains their lips and teeth a deep red — startling at first, but entirely harmless.

A bit of history

The name "Ratenggaro" comes from "rate" (tomb) and "nggaro" (the Garo clan) — a direct legacy of the old clan wars, when the dead were buried on the spot. The village holds around 300 ancient tombs, some right at the ocean's edge. These stone tombs aren't frozen relics: families sometimes save for years, even decades, to give someone who has died a burial befitting their rank — the more imposing the tomb, the more it speaks to the family's standing. The houses follow a similar logic: the taller the roof (up to 15–20 metres here), the higher the family's social status — the lower levels house livestock, the upper levels hold harvests and sacred objects. Each house can shelter up to 50 members of an extended family; during harvest season, most residents head out to the fields, leaving only the elders and children in the village.

Getting there

Reachable by scooter or car from Tambolaka (about 45 minutes) along a coastal road in generally good condition, with a final stretch of sandy track.

Visit quietly, and always ask before photographing a specific person or house. A small contribution is sometimes requested at the village entrance — that's normal, and it supports the community.
