Kampung Ratenggaro

A traditional village facing the ocean, at the mouth of a river.

Home/Discover Sumba/West/Kampung Ratenggaro

Why this place

Ratenggaro is one of the most photographed villages in Sumba, and considered the oldest on the island: houses with conical thatched roofs - called "Uma Mbatangu" - sometimes fifteen to twenty meters high, aligned directly facing the Indian Ocean. These are not reconstructions - families still live there according to an ancestral social order, and megalithic tombs stand between the houses, witnesses of a relationship with the ancestors still living here. The inhabitants belong to the Kodi people, with their own language and traditions; many still chew areca nuts and betel leaves daily, a custom that tints their lips and teeth an intense red — surprising at first, but perfectly innocuous.

A little history

The name "Ratenggaro" comes from "rate" (grave) and "nggaro" (the clan of Garo) — a direct legacy of the clan wars of the past, when the dead were buried on site. The village has around 300 ancient tombs, some directly by the ocean. These stone tombs are not frozen remains: families sometimes save for years, even decades, to be able to offer a deceased a burial worthy of his rank - the more imposing the tomb, the more it testifies to the status of the family. The architecture of the houses follows a similar symbolism: the higher the roof (up to 15-20 meters here), the more it signals the social rank of the family - the lower levels shelter the livestock, the upper levels the crops and sacred objects. Each house can accommodate up to 50 members of the same extended family; during the harvest season, most of the inhabitants go to work in the fields and leave only the elders and the children in the village.

How to get there

Accessible by scooter or car from Tambolaka (around 45 minutes) via a coastal road generally in good condition, with a final portion on a sand track.
