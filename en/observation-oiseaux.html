Bird watching

Nearly 200 species recorded, including 7 endemic to the island — a sanctuary still little-known to ornithologists.

Why Sumba for bird watching

Sumba remains largely under the radar of Indonesia's birdwatching tours, overshadowed by better-known destinations like Flores or Papua. However, the island is home to more than 200 species of birds, including around thirty endemic species and subspecies — present only here and on a few neighboring islands.

Species not to be missed

Sumba boobook owl — rare and discreet, nocturnal sighting Sumba buttonquail (Sumba buttonquail) — vulnerable species Sumba fruit-dove (red-naped fruit-dove) — vulnerable species Sumba hornbill (Sumba hornbill) — vulnerable species, emblematic of the island Sumba sunbird, Sumba myzomela, Sumba dove — more common endemics, visible in the early hours of the day

Where to observe

The still preserved forests of the center of the island, particularly around the national parks of Manupeu Tanah Daru and Laiwangi Wanggameti - known together as MaTaLaWa -, concentrate the greatest diversity: more than 80 species recorded at Manupeu Tanah Daru, including the yellow-crested cockatoo, and 77 species at Laiwangi Wanggameti, which is also home to Mount Wanggameti, the highest point on the island. The Sumba hornbill (Rangkong Sumba) reaches its largest population in the Manupeu sector. The mangrove areas of the South-East, near Melolo, complement an outing with different fauna - waterbirds in particular.

How is it organized

We offer this activity as a day trip, not as a multi-day circuit dedicated to birdwatching — a more accessible option for discovering this little-known side of Sumba without spending an entire stay there. Departure early in the morning (around 5:30 a.m.) to the areas richest in species, on the edge of the Lewa forests and the Manupeu Tanah Daru National Park, with a local guide who knows the areas where the most sought-after species are present. Easily combined with a hike in the Center or our boat trip in the South-East.
