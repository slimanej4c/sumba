Discover Sumba West — Kodi

Kodi's Pasola

The most intense and traditional ritual combat on horseback on the island, on the western tip of Sumba.

Home/Discover Sumba/West/Pasola de Kodi

Why this place

Kodi is a district in Sumba Barat Daya regency (Southwest Sumba) — the same regency as Tambolaka and its airport. It is the westernmost part of this regency, beyond Bondokodi, the largest village in the sector, and well beyond Ratenggaro. Pasola is held every year, between February and March, in four traditional districts: Lamboya, Kodi, Wanokaka and Gaura. The precise dates are never fixed in advance — they are chosen by the Ratos (traditional priests) according to the cycles of the full moon, and can vary from one district to another in the same year. That of Kodi is unanimously considered one of the most intense and authentic of the four, worn by riders renowned throughout the island for their skill.

The Nyale ritual, before the fight

The Pasola is not triggered on a fixed date: it follows the appearance of the Nyale, marine worms which come up to the beaches on a specific night, predicted by traditional priests according to the lunar calendar. Their abundance and color are interpreted as an omen for the coming harvest. It is only once this ritual has been completed, generally in February or March, that the mounted combat can begin.

How to attend

The exact dates are only announced a few weeks in advance — it's impossible to plan them well in advance. If your travel dates fall in February-March, contact us: we follow local announcements and can organize travel to Kodi when the time comes.

Good to know

Pasola involves the ritual use of assegais and can cause slight injuries, considered a good omen by the participants - the fight is witnessed from the edges of the field, never in the center. An experience to be lived with respect, not as a show organized for visitors.
