Pantai Marosi Beach

A beach of rocks sculpted by erosion, at low tide only.

Home/Discover Sumba/West/Pantai Beach Marosi

Why this place

Pantai Marosi is a spectacular beach, sculpted by erosion into arches and stone overhangs — but only visible at low tide: at high tide, it disappears almost entirely underwater. Once on the sand, the view opens onto a small village perched high up, and it is not uncommon to see buffaloes coming down to bathe in the sea at the end of the day.

A little history

Marosi is also a surf spot referenced on an Indonesian scale — a reef break which produces solid straight lines, capable of rising well above the head in big swells. But the site retains a real local character: at low tide, we come across as many villagers coming to collect crabs and squid on the flat as surfers in the water.

How to get there

Access is on foot from the neighboring village, passing through local houses. There is no official route to the beach itself yet — park at the village and walk down.

Good to know

It is customary, although not obligatory, to buy a coconut on site from the inhabitants of the village as a thank you for passing through. In the immediate vicinity, a few minutes on foot or by scooter, the small beach of Dassang completes the visit well for those who want to extend the coastal stroll.
