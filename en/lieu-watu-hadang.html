Discover Sumba South

Watu Hadang Village

Near Melolo — the place where Sumbanese architecture stops being an image and becomes a real system to understand.

Carved stone photographed at Watu Hadang — photo: Mr. Barak / Collectie Wereldmuseum — CC BY-SA 3.0, image cropped by site display.

Home/Discover Sumba/South/Watu Hadang

Why this place

At Watu Hadang, the logic of traditional houses (Uma Mbatangu) becomes readable: the central tower, tall and pointed, above the hearth, is not just a symbol of social rank — it also serves as a chimney, evacuating smoke and hot air from the fire to make the interior livable in the middle of the day. The higher the tip, the better the draw.

In front of the houses, a few steps from the doors, stand the tombs - carved stone slabs, buffalo horns, human and animal motifs. The dead remain at the heart of everyday life rather than relegated to the sidelines, which reflects local Marapu belief.

On site, one can usually watch a demonstration of yarn dyeing — the plants and roots used to color the yarn before weaving, the first step in ikat, rarely shown to visitors.

How to get there

Right next to Melolo, in the South-East of the island.

What to do there

Observe the architectural logic of pointed roofs (chimney + social status) See a yarn dyeing demonstration for ikat Leave with a piece purchased directly from the family

Good to know

Each house has two separate doors: the one on the left is reserved for the family, the one on the right is for visitors. Systematically taking the right door is a small mark of respect which greatly changes the welcome received.
