Our story

Who arewe?

Two Parisians who were not looking for Sumba — and who never really left.

Home/Who are we?

In 2010, one of us set foot in Bali for the first time, in search of an authenticity that everyone was already talking about.

At the time, the island still kept its promises: dirt tracks, rice fields without fences, beaches without rows of deckchairs. Enough to leave a memory that makes you want to return there one day.

Fifteen years later, in 2025, return to Bali — this time as a couple, for several weeks. The shock was immediate, but not what we expected: widespread over-tourism, beaches privatized by beach clubs, rice fields become selfie backdrops. The island we came to no longer really existed in the same way.

So we looked at the map, and we went to look elsewhere - other Indonesian islands, in search of what we had originally come to look for. It was a bit by chance that we landed in Sumba, without any particular expectations. An island twice the size of Bali, but populated by barely 800,000 inhabitants compared to 4.5 million – and above all, light years away from its tourist numbers.

And there, the slap. A real one - even stronger than that of Bali in 2010. Weeks like we have never experienced while traveling.

Villages where ancestral rites are still practiced without staging for visitors. Roads where we come across more horses than rental scooters. Waterfalls that you still have to look for, rather than following an arrow sign. An island that has not – or not yet – been developed for tourism.

We decided to stay there as long as necessary, to learn the terrain, to make the right local contacts — drivers, guides, families who open their village out of trust rather than through transaction. Sumba Nusantara was born from this meeting: an agency run by two French people who fell in love with an island that they were not looking for, with the desire to introduce it to others without distorting it.
