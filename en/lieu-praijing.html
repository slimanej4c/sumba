Praijing Village

Dozens of traditional houses perched on a hill.

Photo: Fakhri Anindita / Wikimedia Commons — CC BY-SA 4.0, image cropped by site display.

Home/Discover Sumba/West/Praijing Village

Why this place

Perched on a hill on the outskirts of Waikabubak, Praijing brings together dozens of traditional houses with pointed roofs, some several generations old, rebuilt identically after a fire a few years ago. Its proximity to Waikabubak makes it one of the easiest villages to fit into a day, without sacrificing authenticity.

Each house rests on four pillars, which symbolically represent the grandparents of the family. The immense space under the pointed roof, often invisible from the outside, is considered the place where the souls of the deceased join the gods — much more than just a granary, this space carries a central spiritual meaning in Marapu belief.

A little history

Praijing offers one of the rare viewpoints where you can see a traditional village in its entirety, from the hill above it. Some of the houses were rebuilt identically after a fire a few years ago — a reminder that these villages remain alive and inhabited, not frozen like open-air museums. In 2026, the village was awarded the ASEAN Sustainable Tourism Award, official recognition of its respectful tourism model and the preservation of its heritage.

How to get there

Just minutes from the center of Waikabubak, accessible by scooter, car or even on foot from some accommodation in town.
